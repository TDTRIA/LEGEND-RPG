Devlog 013 · Release Catch-Up · November 2, 2026

From Road Rework to a Living RPG Loop

The last public devlog described the early Ashmere and Old Road rework. Since then, the project has changed substantially. This entry is a catch-up on the work that now defines the production build, LEGEND: The Awakening v0.30.4.

Play LEGENDDevelopment DispatchDetailed Changelog

1. The first session became a story

The old title and menu work grew into a clearer fantasy-RPG entry point. The new-player route now includes three mandatory annals, an unnamed voice shown as ???, a memory-led traveler creator, and the dead-lantern opening on the Old Road. Captain Oric and the Blackroot Wolf encounter teach the production combat flow rather than a separate tutorial mock-up.

2. Ashmere became a working home

Ashmere's hub and services connect jobs, people, trading, equipment, crafting, recovery, records, and expedition preparation. The first return from the road hands the player's report to Mara and can move the Bell investigation forward. The goal is a town that has a purpose in the loop, not just a screen with links.

3. The Old Road became a journey

The current expedition uses five stages, route branches, named discoveries, camp reports, a field journal and map, time, Road Pressure, and recurring tactical encounters. The road is not meant to be completely safe: a careful route still meets danger after two travel stretches, while higher Pressure can make later threats worse.

Settlement and progression were tuned to slow down early power growth. A baseline completed five-stage trip at 20 Pressure yields 80 gold and 33 XP. A Road Token is a 65% completion roll; an early return does not receive that completion reward. Bell Fragments also have limited chances rather than guaranteed pickups.

4. Combat and progression became more legible

Combat keeps the existing turn-based commands and enemy intent patterns, with readable wind-ups, guard breaks, stagger openings, and results. XP, gear, consumables, and saved attribute points use the existing progression and player record. The expedition result screen and level-up allocation are designed to feel like part of the game world, while retaining the current save contract.

5. Greyfen became the next frontier

Greyfen now has some local quests, resource and equipment progression, marsh routes, the Ferry Nets contract, and an optional Silted Storehouse detour. It is still incomplete: its full service loop and story resolution need work, and Ironpass is future content. The project is deliberately not calling the first three towns finished while those gates remain open.

Where the project stands

The live browser game is desktop-first with mobile compatibility and is actively in development. The current priority is to keep the first journey coherent, correct the public-facing information, validate the live production build, and complete each region as a playable loop before opening the next one.

The production Phaser world renderer is now active, and the approved migration is underway one playable surface at a time. The existing save key and player record remain the source of truth.
