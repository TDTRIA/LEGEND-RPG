Devlog 014 · Audio Identity & Engine Transition · November 3, 2026

The Sound of the Realm

The next improvement to LEGEND: The Awakening is something every player hears. The recurring gameplay music needs a stronger identity. The current theme does not carry the world we are building, and adding more sound effects around it will not solve that.

Today’s work is focused on giving LEGEND a consistent sound: music with a recognizable melody, places that sound different from one another, and effects that feel like they belong to the same medieval-fantasy world.

A full location sound library

We generated a 48-asset original collection for the game: 13 thirty-second ambience loops and 35 short sound effects. The ambience is tailored to places already represented in the game rather than using one generic wind loop everywhere.

  • Ashmere: the town square, Inn, and Forge.
  • The opening: the title scene, the three-annal prologue, and the traveler's memory sequence.
  • The Old Road: Blackroot forest, High Ridge, Reed Cut, and a roadside camp.
  • Greyfen: the marsh and working docks.
  • Bell Cellar: a damp subterranean ambience with a distant bronze resonance.

The one-shot effects cover sword swings and impacts, blocking and parrying, spellcasting and healing, creature calls, loot and Bell Fragment discoveries, level-ups, quest completion, footsteps across different ground, doors, the forge, maps, markets, and other town interactions.

Integrated and deployed The 48-asset collection is merged into production main and included in the successful GitHub Pages deployment. It provides the current location ambience and generated gameplay sound library; the remaining pass is subjective listening, mix/loop verification, and wiring the remaining one-shot cues to their corresponding game events.

The new main theme now runs through the realm

The soundtrack should have a musical identity, not just a background drone. The direction is a memorable recurring motif carried by expressive cello and strings, restrained horns, subtle medieval folk instrumentation, and occasional bronze-bell accents. It should feel adventurous and weathered, melancholic without becoming hopeless, and comfortable to hear during a long session.

The original instrumental score has been rendered as a roughly 10½-minute loop and is now integrated in the production game. A shared audio director keeps the main theme continuous across exploration, battle, and results while changing the underlying ambience as the player moves between the title, Ashmere, the Old Road, Greyfen, and the Bell Cellar. It also retains the existing sound toggle and prevents competing music loops from stacking.

Integrated · listening QA pending The deployed build now uses the replacement theme and location ambience. The remaining verification is a real-browser listening pass for audible playback, relative levels, the loop seam, and long-session comfort; the automated browser suite is still running.

One world, not a stack of menus

There is also an important engine milestone. Phaser 4.1 is now loaded in the production game and renders the persistent world layer, environment atmosphere, transitions, and Ashmere landmarks. That is the start of the production migration the project is moving toward, not the end of it.

Most menus, dialogue, combat rules, expedition logic, account flows, and save handling still use the existing browser modules. Those systems will move into Phaser incrementally, one complete playable surface at a time, with the current game remaining playable and the existing saves and gameplay rules preserved.

What happens next

  1. Listen to the deployed theme and location ambience in the browser, confirm levels and loop seam, and connect the remaining generated effects to their matching successful game actions.
  2. Keep the main theme continuous through combat and results; assess whether a separate related battle arrangement is actually needed after listening in the game.
  3. Move real gameplay surfaces into Phaser while keeping save data, quests, progression, combat balance, and accessibility intact.
  4. Continue completing Greyfen's town services, local economy, story, expeditions, and return loop. It remains an active partial region, not a finished town.

LEGEND is still an in-development browser RPG. This update describes work in progress and does not announce a new game version or a finished 1.0 release.
